1 Problem Selection & Operational Context

Microdrama apps (ReelShort, DramaBox, Kuku) acquire most of their paying users through vertical video ads posted on TikTok, Instagram, and YouTube. Releasing a 50-episode show weekly makes manual promo selection an operational bottleneck: video editors spend hours watching raw episodes by hand to identify 20–60s hooks. I selected Problem 1 because it addresses this direct revenue and spend bottleneck with rigorous, checkable constraints rather than open-ended speculation.

What Makes a Winning Microdrama Promo?
  • 3-Second Scroll-Stopping Hook: Immediate high-tension beat (altar betrayal, public slap, drawn weapon) within $\le 3.0$ seconds.
  • Unresolved Cliffhanger: Cuts abruptly at high tension before revelation, compelling viewers to download the app.
Broadcast & Platform Requirements
  • Standalone Clarity: Strangers must instantly understand the central stakes without needing prior backstory.
  • Safe Acoustic Cut Points: Strict 20–60s duration, cutting precisely during natural dialogue pauses (never clipping words).
2 Solution Overview: HookScene Studio

HookScene is a full-stack promo discovery engine and editorial review desk. It automates candidate hunting while keeping the human editor in the loop. By employing a neuro-symbolic architecture, it divides labor strictly: LLMs comprehend narrative nuances, character dynamics, and dramatic tension, while deterministic Python code computes timestamps, validates duration (20–60s), ensures hook immediacy ($\le 3$s), and prevents audio boundary clipping. It generates both Continuous Single Takes and Paced Jump-Cut Montages with transparent decision traces.

3 How the System Works: 6-Stage Execution Pipeline
  • Step 1: Screenplay Ingestion & Tokenization: Ingests .txt episodes, .zip archives, or .srt captions. Parses raw text into discrete Line entities with unique line IDs (e.g., E02_L004). Calculates cut_ok safe boundaries where speech $\ge 0.1$s is silent.
  • Step 2: Narrative Arc & Anti-Spoiler Shield: Performs a global summary pass across all 50 episodes. Maps characters, romantic arcs, and betrayal triggers. Consensus voting identifies late climax episodes (Eps 44–50) to suppress spoiler candidates.
  • Step 3: Dramatic Peak Hunter: AI analyzes episode dialogue for high-retention turning points. A deterministic keyword heuristic operates simultaneously as a safety backstop. Crucial rule: The AI returns only Line IDs, never arbitrary timestamps.
  • Step 4: Deterministic Code Quality Gates: Python converts Line IDs into verified timestamps and validates 3 invariants: (a) duration 20–60s, (b) hook onset $\le 3.0$s, and (c) cliffhanger cut occurs before resolving lines. Fails auto-calculate legal adjustment windows.
  • Step 5: Multi-Rubric Scoring & Listwise Ranking: Dual judges score 5 rubrics (Hook 3s, Cliffhanger, Standalone Clarity, Intensity, Plot Relevance). An attention proxy adds voice immediacy and motion scores. Candidates are ranked via multi-pass Borda count.
  • Step 6: Montage Synthesis & NLE Timeline Export: Combines intra-scene and multi-episode moments to eliminate dead pauses for short-form social feeds. Generates industry-standard CMX 3600 EDL, Final Cut XML, CSV, and JSON deliverables.

5 Engineering Approach & Scalability

The Neuro-Symbolic Boundary (AI Proposes, Code Disposes): Asking LLMs to directly output time codes results in hallucinated arithmetic and mid-word cuts. HookScene restricts the model to citing immutable line IDs. Python code computes all cut points from ground-truth screenplay timings, enforcing duration constraints and acoustic zero-crossings deterministically.

Parallel Scalability

Ingestion and candidate extraction run asynchronously across episodes. Ingesting and evaluating all 50 episodes finishes in under 3 minutes on the fast profile (~103 API calls).

Provider Swapping

Decoupled provider architecture via config.yaml. Switch instantly between Gemini Flash, Groq (Llama-3), or company API gateways without touching application logic.

Cost Telemetry

SHA-256 disk cache prevents redundant re-queries. Running an entire 50-episode show costs $0.00 on Gemini Free Tier or ~$0.18 (₹16) on paid high-throughput tiers.

7 Real Limitations & The Role of Human Review
Where the AI System Has Blind Spots
  • Text vs Video Gap: Evaluates screenplay lines, not rendered video footage or actor delivery; a subtle visual glare cannot be measured by text alone.
  • Lower-Rank Score Drift: Top 5–10 candidates are highly stable, but candidates ranked 20–30 show 0.5–1.0 score variance across runs.
  • Timestamp Alignment: Assumes screenplays match final video cut timings without production discrepancies.
Why the Human Editor Remains Essential

HookScene acts as an editorial multiplier, not an autonomous black box. Instead of watching 50 episodes (2+ hours), the editor reviews 10 pre-validated candidates in 5 minutes.

Human review is critical to verify comedic timing, actor charisma, cultural sensitivities, and platform-specific advertising compliance policies.

Real Debugging Case: The Episode Number Collision Failure

Failure: During multi-file upload testing with custom test episodes (test-episode-01.txt, test-episode-06.txt, test-episode-07.txt), an AI-generated regex in parse.py strictly expected EPISODE NUMBER: Episode <num>. Because the files had headers like TEST-01 and Test Episode 06, the parser failed to match, defaulting all 3 files to Episode 00. In Python's episode dictionary em = {e.num: e}, the last file silently overwrote the earlier episodes, resulting in promos being produced only from the final file.

Identification & Fix: I caught the error in the Review Desk when all cards displayed Ep. 00 and only 2 promos appeared. I replaced the rigid regex with a flexible digit pattern matching any prefix (TEST-01, EP-06, Episode 7), added a fallback to extract the number from the filename, and added an automatic de-collision indexer in load_show so episodes never overwrite each other.
